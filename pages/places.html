01 · Philippines

Calamba & Biñan, Laguna

1861–1877

José Rizal was born in Calamba, Laguna, on 19 June 1861. His family managed agricultural land there. He began his schooling at home before attending school in Biñan, where he studied Latin.

Why it mattered: Calamba shaped his early world: family, land, and the inequality around tenant farming that later appeared in his letters and petitions.

02 · Philippines

Manila & Intramuros

1872–1896

After Biñan, Rizal studied at the Ateneo Municipal de Manila and the University of Santo Tomas. He later returned to organize La Liga Filipina in 1892 and was tried in Manila in 1896.

Why it mattered: The city is where his education, reform work, and final legal battle converged. His memoir recalls a house in Intramuros during his student years.

03 · Philippines

Bagumbayan / Luneta

1896 · 1912

Rizal was executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan, now Luneta/Rizal Park, on 30 December 1896. His farewell poem was written in Fort Santiago before his death.

Why it mattered: The site became the center of public memory. His remains were moved in 1912 to the monument beneath which they now rest.

04 · Philippines

Dapitan, Zamboanga del Norte

1892–1896

Exiled in Dapitan, Rizal treated patients, taught boys, worked on water and farming projects, and built a relief map of Mindanao with local students.

Why it mattered: Exile did not end his work. Dapitan produced medicine, teaching, art, letters, and some of his most reflective writing.

05 · Nearby

Hong Kong

1891 · 1897

Rizal settled in Hong Kong in late 1891 and began a third novel there in both Tagalog and Spanish. His farewell poem was printed there in 1897 without the title he had given it.

Why it mattered: Hong Kong links his unfinished fiction to the afterlife of his most famous poem.

06 · Abroad

Barcelona, Spain

1882 · 1889

Rizal reached Spain in 1882. Barcelona became associated with La Solidaridad, the reform newspaper founded by Filipinos there in 1889, and his essay “Amor Patrio” carries a Barcelona dateline in June 1882.

Why it mattered: The city connects his early reform writing with the newspaper that carried later essays to readers in the Philippines.

Amor Patrio Filipinas dentro de Cien Años
07 · Abroad

Madrid, Spain

1882–1884

At the Universidad Central de Madrid, Rizal continued his medical studies and earned his medical degree in 1884. He also wrote the essay “Revista de Madrid.”

Why it mattered: Madrid gave him the professional training he had left home to pursue and a base for observing Spain at close range.

08 · Abroad

Paris, France

1880s · 1890

Rizal studied ophthalmology in Paris and saw his annotated edition of Morga’s Sucesos de las Islas Filipinas printed there in 1890.

Why it mattered: Paris links his medical specialization with his historical research into the Philippines.

09 · Abroad

Heidelberg, Germany

1886

While training as an eye doctor in Germany, Rizal wrote “A las flores de Heidelberg” in Heidelberg in the spring of 1886.

Why it mattered: The poem turns a European city into a message home, carrying greetings to the people and country he missed.

A las flores de Heidelberg Own Words
10 · Abroad

Berlin, Germany

1887

Noli Me Tángere was printed in Berlin in 1887 with a loan from Máximo Viola. That same year Rizal presented a treatise on Tagalog verse to the Ethnographical Society of Berlin.

Why it mattered: Berlin is tied to both his first novel and his effort to explain Filipino language and culture to European scholars.

Noli Me Tángere Arte Métrica del Tagalog
11 · Abroad

Ghent, Belgium

1891

El Filibusterismo was printed in Ghent in 1891 with help from Valentín Ventura. Rizal dedicated it to Gómez, Burgos, and Zamora, the three priests executed in 1872.

Why it mattered: The darker sequel was made in exile and carries the memory of 1872 into its dedication.

12 · Abroad

London, England

1888–1889

In London, Rizal copied Morga’s history by hand at the British Museum, wrote his 1889 Tagalog letter to the young women of Malolos, and published “Specimens of Tagal Folklore.”

Why it mattered: London brought together archival research, Tagalog writing, and folklore collection in one period of his travels.
