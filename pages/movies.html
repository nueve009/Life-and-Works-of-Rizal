Rizal beyond the page

Rizal on Screen

Filmmakers have played Rizal as a student, a lover, an exile, a martyr, and even a question. These films and documentaries put him in front of the camera instead of only on the printed page.

These are interpretations

A film is not a primary source. Dates, dialogue, and motivations may be dramatized, and even documentaries make editorial choices. Use the source links on each card and the Sources page to verify anything before citing it. The card pictures are original illustrations, not official posters; where a preview exists, the play button opens the trailer on its host site.

Where to begin: José Rizal (1998) for the whole life, Rizal sa Dapitan (1997) for the exile years, and Bayaning 3rd World for a playful argument about the legend.

Silent biopic

1912 · Directed by Edward Gross · Philippines

La vida de José Rizal

Rizal: Honorio Lopez

Often cited as the first feature film produced in the Philippines, this silent picture traces Rizal from his childhood in Calamba to his execution at Bagumbayan.

Postwar biopic

1956 · Directed by Ramon A. Estella · Philippines

Ang Buhay at Pag-ibig ni Dr. Jose Rizal

Rizal: Eddie del Mar

A three-hour life-and-loves epic that became closely associated with its lead actor. Del Mar’s performance won the FAMAS Best Actor award for 1956.

1997 The exile years Original illustration ▶ Watch preview
Exile drama

1997 · Directed by Tikoy Aguiluz · Philippines

Rizal sa Dapitan

Rizal: Albert Martinez

A quiet, intimate look at the four exile years: Rizal builds a school, treats patients, maps the coast, and falls in love with Josephine Bracken. It won twelve awards at the 1997 Manila Film Festival.

1998 Centennial epic Original illustration ▶ Official trailer
Centennial epic

1998 · Directed by Marilou Diaz-Abaya · Philippines

José Rizal

Rizal: Cesar Montano (young Rizal: Dominic Guinto)

The best-known Rizal biopic tells his story in flashbacks from prison to execution, weaving in scenes from his novels. It swept the 1998 Metro Manila Film Festival and travelled to Berlin, Toronto, and Chicago.

1999 / 2000 A question, not an answer Original illustration ▶ Watch preview
Mockumentary

1999 / 2000 · Directed by Mike de Leon · Philippines

Bayaning 3rd World

Rizal: Joel Torre

Two fictional filmmakers interview Rizal’s family, Josephine Bracken, a Jesuit priest, and Rizal himself. The black-and-white film asks what can and cannot be known about a national hero; it won six Gawad Urian awards, including Best Picture.

1999 Author meets character Original illustration No verified preview
Fictional encounter

1999 · Directed by Mario O’Hara · Philippines

Sisa

Rizal: Gardo Versoza

Writer-director Mario O’Hara imagines Rizal meeting Sisa, the fictional mother from Noli Me Tangere. It is a deliberate blurring of author, character, and history rather than a conventional biography.

Supporting role

2014 · Directed by Enzo Williams · Philippines

Bonifacio: Ang Unang Pangulo

Rizal: Jericho Rosales

This Bonifacio-centred drama begins with the 1872 executions and the founding of La Liga Filipina. Rizal appears briefly as the reformist whose arrest helps push Bonifacio toward revolution.

Supporting role

2023 · Directed by Pepe Diokno · Philippines

GomBurZa

Rizal: Khalil Ramos (young Rizal: Alexandre Lucas Martin)

The film centres on Gomez, Burgos, and Zamora, but Rizal frames their story: as a boy he hears their fate, and in the epilogue he dedicates El Filibusterismo to their memory.

Documentary

1995 · Philippine Centennial production

Jose Rizal: The Pride of the Malay Rice

Portrayed by: Pen Medina

One of two centennial documentaries in which Pen Medina took the role of Rizal. It sits alongside the better-known feature films as an example of screen Rizal made for study and commemoration.

Documentary

2012 · Directed by Paolo Abella · Philippines

Jose Rizal: The First Hero

Focus: the man behind the monument

Part of a series on Philippine national heroes, this documentary aims to show a more human side of Rizal before his death. It was also released as part of a GMA News and Public Affairs Rizal collection.

Different from adaptations. Movies based on Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo bring Rizal’s stories to the screen, but they do not usually portray Rizal himself. Those are listed with his writing on the Works page, not here.

Release years can differ between a festival screening, a first public showing, and a theatrical release. Bayaning 3rd World, for example, is commonly dated 1999 in festival listings and 2000 in Philippine release records. Follow the source links to check the version you are using.
